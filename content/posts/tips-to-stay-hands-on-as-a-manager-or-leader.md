As you grow in your career from Software Engineer to Tech Lead or Manager, you spend more time managing, reviewing code and mentoring people, so you get less time for coding.

So the question is: how do you stay hands-on with coding? Here are some tips you can follow.

1. Always remember that as a hands-on manager, you are supposed to solve tech debt, not ship features. Picking up features means that when you get pulled into something else, which you will, you put the delivery of that feature at risk.

2. Instead, pick tasks that are not part of the product's core delivery, or that don't need consistent effort across weeks. It is almost certain you won't find that much dedicated time, and if you take on that kind of work, your team might get blocked because of you.

3. Don't shy away from fixing bugs, finishing documentation, or fixing that typo in the code that everyone missed. The goal is to contribute to the areas that are easy to miss.

4. Try not to be the 100x developer you once were.

5. Try to get involved in pair programming with a junior developer, and let them lead the story. Guide them on better practices while you do. It will motivate them. They will thank you for all the guidance, and you will thank them for reminding you how it feels to be there again: learning, making mistakes, and seeing that it is still OK to make those mistakes over and over again.
